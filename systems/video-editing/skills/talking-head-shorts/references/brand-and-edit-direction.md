# Brand and edit direction for talking-head shorts

Apply this with the creative standard on every founder talking-head short. It is an editing brief, not a claim that the bundled kit already implements it. Default to approximately 30 seconds if the spoken idea supports it.

## Fast, compelling editorial rhythm

- First second: open on a truthful, strong spoken hook and a readable headline; land a story-specific visual pattern interrupt and a sonic accent without masking the first words. Frame zero should already show the subject or subject-relevant visual. No empty logo intro or invented promise.
- Cut tightly on phrases, breaths or changes in thought. Remove dead time while preserving meaning, qualifications, natural voice and continuity. Use quick shot changes, varied close and wider framings, brief detail shots, purposeful inserts, motion and occasional holds. The older video style guide observed roughly two-second cuts in reference footage; treat that as a starting point, not a measured retention result or a mandatory metronome. Slow down for an important or complex idea.
- Aim for visually rewarding moments: a meaningful reveal, striking real close-up, tactile action, sharp proof, a contrast between ideas, a well-timed graphic or a punch-in matched to the thought. Vary shot scale and energy. Avoid repetitive zooms, fake urgency, flicker, effects that obscure words and a distracting change on every beat. Do not amplify soft footage beyond usable quality.
- Build a beat sheet with source in/out, dialogue, shot, B-roll, movement, on-screen text and sound. Each insert must add context or show what is being discussed. Edit B-roll into and out of the spoken idea; keep it visible long enough to read. Return to the founder when expression or trust matters. Use moving footage or screenshots when available, not generic cards pretending to be evidence.
- Use founder-owned captures and recordings or explicitly cleared material with known licences. Do not download third-party footage, music, fonts, models or effects without founder approval and a stated licence. Never show confidential client material without approval. Do not invent screenshots, proof, claims or a CTA. If assets are missing, flag the gap and request or plan specific shots; distinguish an editorial illustration from real evidence.

## Brand system: personal brand and AdvrtizX

The founder-designated source is `the founder-designated AdvrtizX style extraction (request the latest approved copy)` (27 October 2026). It is a reference extraction, not a completed brand guideline. Check for later approved guidance before each project. Use it for the personal brand and AdvrtizX unless the brief specifies a different approved system.

- Colour roles: signal red `#F20B14`, deep red `#CF0009`, white `#FFFFFF`, ink `#0B0B0A`, muted grey `#4D4D4D`. Red and white lead; use ink for hierarchy and limited neutral support. Avoid unrelated decorative hues. Source footage and truthful third-party material retain their original colours.
- Type: Impact for condensed, bold display hooks; intended Geist Sans for headlines, supporting copy and readable captions; intended Geist Mono for restrained labels or indexes. Use short lines, hierarchy and ample contrast. The extracted site had broken Geist font loads: verify locally available font files and their licence before use; do not silently download or claim the rendered type is Geist if it is a fallback. If unavailable, clearly record the actual fallback and request approved font assets. Never copy a broken font load into a new edit.
- Motion language: intentional transform and opacity, decisive arrivals, small offset or slight rotation, ease-out `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)` as a starting point. The source site uses 0.25 to 0.8 seconds for transforms; adapt to the spoken beat rather than presenting those values as performance evidence. Use bold typographic reveals, purposeful crop or mask reveals, and graphic movement that follows an idea. Avoid gratuitous shaking, flashes and motion that blocks the face or proof.
- Compose using strong type, clean red or white fields and a clear focal point. Keep captions off the eyes and mouth, within platform-safe areas, and readable on a phone. White on signal red measures about 4.34:1 in the source extraction; for small text use an accessible contrast pairing such as white on deep red or ink on white. Confirm contrast and legibility in actual frames.
- The older video style guide uses Inter and Playfair Display and the sprint proposal recommends `#E90202`; the AdvrtizX v5 site also uses Inter Tight and `#E90202`. These are different source systems, not interchangeable approved tokens. The later founder-designated extraction above is the working reference for personal brand visuals; the final red value was previously flagged for confirmation. Record which source a project uses, and do not claim the conflicting red was finally approved. Do not restyle another account to this system without its applicable direction; 3RDWRONG's identity route remains unapproved in the sprint record.

## Sound and quality gate

Create a cohesive soundtrack from original or licensed approved material: clear dialogue first, room tone under cuts, a hook accent, and varied but deliberate sound effects tied to real transitions, actions and reveals. Use a music bed where suitable and cleared, mix and duck it under speech by ear, and use an original SFX stand-in if no approved track exists. Note when music is missing. Do not invent a licence. Review the complete final export with sound, including phone-size playback, and verify loudness and true peak. Inspect cut points, captions against speech, spelling, asset rights, brand choices, first-second hook, source quality, insert readability and visual continuity. Fix defects and check the new export. If any check was not performed, say so; no false claim of a finished edit.

## Renderer limitations

The existing kit does not yet provide arbitrary moving video inserts, custom keyframes or automatic music ducking. Implement and test these in an isolated copy or use a verified local edit pipeline when needed; do not claim a feature exists because it is specified here. No performance or output quality is guaranteed by this written direction alone.
