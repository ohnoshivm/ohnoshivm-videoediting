# Creative standard: 30-second talking-head proof cut

This is the requested direction for future edits, not a claim that the current renderer already supports every technique. Every timing below is an editorial starting point to test against the footage, not a measured guarantee of retention.

## First second: hook and pattern interrupt

- Pick the sharpest honest phrase or moment from the recording. Keep the visual, the actual spoken idea and sound aligned; do not manufacture a stronger claim.
- At 0.0–1.0 s, pair that line with a clearly legible, unexpected visual change: a punch-in on the actual subject, an abrupt reveal of the real evidence, a dramatic crop that resolves, a short freeze paired with the real audio, or another idea that serves this specific story. The first frame must already communicate a topic. Avoid flashes, disorientation and silent decorative intros.
- Add one well-chosen audio accent or intentional contrast if it supports the visual. Do not mask the first word. Check the opening at normal speed, with sound, and muted on a phone-sized preview.
- By roughly 3–5 s establish the stakes and what the viewer will learn. Through the body reveal a clear payoff; finish on a complete sentence, or a CTA only when the speaker or approved brief supports it. No invented comment keyword or promise.

## Motion and pacing

- Make a beat sheet with a reason for each cut and movement. Vary face, split and visual evidence; change shot size on an idea shift, not on a timer. Keep the face prominent when emotion or trust carries the point.
- Use deliberate movement: e.g. a screenshot pans into the relevant detail, an annotation draws around a real item, type enters on a key word, two real items slide into comparison, or a short piece of relevant authorised video takes over. Use an ease and a readable hold; avoid repetitive zooms, busy transitions, aggressive shake, and unnecessary flashes. Motion must not obscure evidence or words.
- Prioritise clear text and real content above ornaments. Wordmark-only cards can label a beat but cannot replace actual proof. Match visuals to the specific sentence; do not use charts or numbers that are unverified. Prefer original local material or properly licensed/approved assets, never unlicensed downloads.
- Keep meaningful framing within a 9:16 master. Avoid a severe enlargement of low-resolution landscape footage; use a considered layout if the crop is soft. Keep captions off eyes, mouth, hair breakout and platform controls. Preview safe areas per destination; verify dark/light contrast in every layout, including under a moving matte.

## Sound design

- Start with clean, intelligible voice, remove distracting noise conservatively, preserve natural breathing and room tone, and check each cut for clicks or truncated words.
- Choose a coherent palette rather than generic effects on every cut: a hook accent, a reveal whoosh, a tactile click or tick tied to an actual action, and a payoff accent where warranted. Synchronise events to the frame. Use fades to prevent clicks; use silence intentionally.
- Add an original or licensed music bed only if it suits the story and permission covers the use. Set its level and ducking by ear against the dialogue, accounting for phone speakers. Do not invent a licence or download without approval.
- Finish toward −14 LUFS integrated and true peak at or below −1 dBTP unless the destination or brief requires otherwise. These are targets; measure the exported file. Do not treat sample peak as true peak. Listen on the final file and adjust if speech is masked or effects are harsh.

## Visual proof and verification

For each beat record: spoken words; visual asset; source and permission; intended claim; in/out frame; movement; sound. Check screenshot content is genuine, current to the recorded claim, readable and not cropped deceptively. Personal/client material stays private unless approved. Check all displayed figures against sources and speech.

QC must include full playback with sound; exact caption text and timing against audio; hook frame and first second; matte edges and hair; transitions; phone-size previews; text and subject safe zones; source rights; export codec, dimensions, frame rate, loudness and true peak. Provide a concrete defect list, fix it and inspect the revision. If audio playback or independent review is unavailable, mark it unverified and keep the reel as a draft.

## Kit reality

The bundled `edit.json` supports still-image swaps and primitive animations, word/split/graphic layouts, a single light leak, and five named synthetic SFX. It does not provide arbitrary keyed motion, embedded video proof or an automatic ducked music bed. Extend and test an isolated kit or use a verified local editing pipeline for those features; do not claim they are rendered simply because this document requests them. Preserve no-download, zero-spend and project safety constraints.
