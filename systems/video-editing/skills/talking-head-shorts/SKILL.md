# Talking-head shorts: high-craft proof cuts

Turn real founder footage into a compelling, polished vertical reel. Default to about 30 seconds when the source supports a complete idea; adjust only if the brief asks or the story needs it. A good edit begins with a reason to watch, develops one idea with real evidence, and finishes on a complete thought. Do not pad a weak source or make a short excerpt look like a completed 30-second reel.

Read `references/brand-and-edit-direction.md` for brand application, fast cuts and B-roll; `references/creative-standard.md` for the hook, motion, visual and sound direction; `references/edit-schema.md` for actual kit capabilities; and `references/style-bible.md` for the original reference observations. The creative standard overrides older stylistic defaults where they conflict. Reference measurements describe reference edits, not proof of performance. Never copy another creator's recognisable identity or assets.

## Safety, rights and context

Use founder-owned or specifically cleared footage and assets. Do not download third-party footage, music, fonts, models or sound effects without an explicit licence and the founder's approval. Do not fabricate proof, numbers, screenshots, quotations or a CTA; do not change meaning by cutting mid-thought. Keep actual spoken claims in context, including qualifications and dates. Obtain proof assets from the user or use demonstrably accurate local materials; if missing, make a clearly labelled editorial treatment from the spoken idea and flag the missing proof, rather than presenting decoration as evidence. Never replace the founder's voice or likeness with synthetic media. Obtain founder approval for release; never publish, schedule, send or spend.

## Workflow

1. **Select.** Inspect the raw recording and existing transcripts. Find a self-contained, compelling 25–35 second story with a clear change or payoff; respect the requested duration. Review the actual audio, context and likely rights before choosing. If the only available passage is weak or incomplete, say so and identify what footage is needed. Respect existing claim and topic restrictions.
2. **Hook.** Start with the strongest truthful spoken moment. Design one deliberate visual and/or audio pattern interrupt in the first second, tied to the claim: for example an oversized, cropped proof image that resolves into a reveal, a sudden framing change or a meaningful sonic accent. The verbal hook should be intelligible immediately. The first frame must work silently and on a phone. Do not misrepresent the source or make an unearned clickbait promise.
3. **Evidence and assets.** Build a beat sheet: time, spoken claim, supporting screenshot or footage and source/permission, action, motion and sound. Prefer sharp real screenshots or relevant footage over generic wordmark cards. Use readable crops, focus highlights and detail reveals; show enough surrounding context for accuracy. Prepare each asset to fit the frame without avoidable blur or stretched text. If assets are absent, disclose the gap; do not claim a proof-rich cut was made.
4. **Prep carefully.** The bundled `shorts-prep.mjs` may install Whisper and download a model, deletes its work directory, and automatically cuts pauses. Inspect it before running; do not trigger downloads or delete existing data without authorisation. Prefer the existing local whisper.cpp and `ggml-small.en.bin` toolbelt when available. English ASR is a draft: compare every caption and brand name with the actual audio, correct text and word timing, and check that every speech cut preserves sense and has no audible click. Do not trust DTW timestamps as verified onsets. Keep a copy of the raw material intact.
5. **Edit.** Build a fast, varied timeline with purposeful cuts, compelling shot changes and relevant B-roll as detailed in `references/brand-and-edit-direction.md`: motivate movement with a reveal, comparison, detail, emphasis or transition in the story. Use meaningful reframing, keyframed screenshot movement, callouts, staggered text and selective transitions, with hold time for reading. Preserve a calm visual hierarchy and avoid constant zooms, flashes and gratuitous SFX. Per-beat decisions and concrete timing are in `references/creative-standard.md`.
6. **Sound.** Use the `openverse-sfx`, `real-sfx` and `earshot` skills as appropriate to source, cue and assess sound; consult `references/sound-system.md` for precedence, limits and a practical sequence. Use already cleared assets first and do not download unapproved audio. Preserve separate voice/music/SFX stems and use an explicit frame-aligned cue sheet. Clean and level dialogue, repair cuts with room tone, use a licensed or original music bed only if it helps, duck it beneath speech, and layer a restrained palette of relevant SFX on meaningful events. The kit offers five synthetic SFX; this is a starting palette, not a complete sound design. Mix by ear and verify final loudness and true peak; never claim an audio review without hearing the export.
7. **Renderer.** Remotion is for graphics, captions and compositing; use ffmpeg and editorial judgement to cut and reframe footage. The bundled kit supports the fields in `edit-schema.md`; it does not yet implement arbitrary moving video inserts, custom keyframes, or a music-bed mixer. Create and test extensions in an isolated working copy if the brief needs them. Do not pretend an unsupported effect exists or silently install dependencies. Tell the user before restructuring, reinstalling or deleting anything in `the studio Remotion project`.
8. **Check.** Validate the timeline, inspect stills at the hook and each visual beat, then play the entire finished video with sound and review captions against speech. Inspect the matte, transitions, B-roll rights and context, brand typography and colours, phone-size legibility, platform safe areas, framing and actual visual proof. Run `ffprobe` and loudness/true-peak checks. If a validator demands an invented CTA, report that limitation rather than adding a false one. Correct defects and re-render. State clearly which checks could not run; a contact sheet alone does not prove audio or timing quality.
9. **Deliver.** Attach the final local file and note source, duration, outstanding editorial or rights issues and review status. Founder approval and the applicable independent quality review remain required before release. Never call a draft publish-ready when audio, captions, claims, rights or visual defects remain unverified.

## Installed kit and limitations

The kit is in `kit/`; its schema and validator are documented in `references/edit-schema.md`. The existing validator's last-segment CTA requirement is a template limitation, not permission to invent spoken words. The existing style bible contains historical reference measurements; its strict percentages, static-shot and hard-cut rules are not mandatory for this upgraded direction. Preserve the current skill's prep, render and reference-study scripts, but inspect their side effects before running. Script availability is not evidence that the upgraded visual and audio capabilities are implemented.

## Reference study

`bash "$SKILL_DIR/scripts/study-reference.sh" <video> <outdir>` produces probe data and contact sheets; inspect the actual frames. The `watch` skill may upload private footage to a third party, so obtain permission before any such upload. Record observations, measurements and inferences separately. Never copy a reference's footage, identity, copy or proprietary assets.
