# Editing and sound workflow

1. **Ingest and story:** Preserve the raw file. Use the separately cleared local talking-head skill if available (withheld here pending rights confirmation); select a complete, truthful idea and a strong first-second hook. Default to about 30 seconds when the source supports it.
2. **Plan:** Create a beat sheet: source in/out, spoken words, visual treatment, relevant proof or B-roll, planned motion, cue time, sound source and licence. Secure rights and required approvals for footage, screenshots, fonts and audio.
3. **Cut and frame:** Cut at meaningful phrases with FFmpeg or an editor. Keep qualifications and avoid misleading cuts. Create an English ASR draft with the already approved local toolbelt, then have a person correct the transcript and timing by ear.
4. **Build visuals:** Use Remotion for captions, graphics, composition and the brand system. Check the actual approved guidance for the specific account. Avoid exaggerating resolution when the source is soft. Inspect the first second, cuts and phone-safe areas.
5. **Source sound:** Use approved owned audio first. If a sound is missing, `openverse-sfx` can search candidates; inspect the original source and exact licence and record the attribution before any approved download. Some CC licences limit commercial use or adaptation; a search result is not clearance.
6. **Cue and mix:** Use `real-sfx` with explicit typed event times where possible. Put meaningful effects on intentional motion or actions. Keep SFX, music and dialogue as separate stems, do not double the original effects, and leave space for speech. The bundled UI pack is synthetic CC0; the separate studio synth library is not a real-recording library.
7. **Analyse:** If installed and models available, use `earshot` to compare candidates, inspect timeline/sync and critique a mix. Its procedural sound bank creates synthetic effects. It cannot establish provenance or speech content. Its model scores and launch-film comparisons are imperfect references, not proof of quality.
8. **Verify:** Measure the encoded export's loudness and true peak, inspect every meaningful cue and visual beat, check duration/aspect ratio/codec, and review rights/credits. A human listens through the whole mix, checks dialogue and captions, and approves the result. Explicitly report any unchecked item.

Do not force every cut onto a beat, add a sound to every event, or insert effects just to improve a score. Story and intelligibility come first. Source media, model weights, caches and intermediate exports remain private and outside a public Git checkout.
